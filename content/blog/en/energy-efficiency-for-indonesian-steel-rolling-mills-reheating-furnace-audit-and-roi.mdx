## 1. What is a Reheating Furnace & What is Its Purpose in a Steel Mill?

Before diving into audit protocols, plant managers and operational engineers must understand the core thermodynamics and mechanical purpose of the reheating furnace within the rolling line.

### What is a Reheating Furnace?
A **reheating furnace** is a continuous or semi-continuous industrial furnace utilized in hot rolling mills to elevate solid semi-finished steel stock—such as continuously cast billets, blooms, beam blanks, or slabs—from ambient temperature (or hot-charging temperatures of 500°C to 700°C) to the plastic deformation range, typically **1,150°C to 1,250°C**.

Thermally, the furnace consists of three distinct functional chambers:
1. **Preheating Zone:** Utilizes counter-flowing convective heat from hot exhaust gases to raise billet temperature to ~700°C without consuming direct burner fuel.
2. **Heating Zone:** Operates at peak burner intensity (1,250°C–1,320°C chamber temperature) transferring radiant heat to the steel stock.
3. **Soaking Zone:** Operates under gentle, uniform radiant heat (1,180°C–1,220°C) allowing core-to-surface temperature equilibration across the billet cross-section.

### What is the Purpose of a Reheat Furnace in a Steel Mill?
The primary purpose of a reheat furnace in a steel mill is twofold:
* **Lowering Flow Stress & Yield Strength:** Steel at 1,200°C requires less than 15% of the mechanical rolling force needed at ambient temperatures, reducing the electric motor power consumption of roughing and intermediate stands and extending rolling roll lifespan.
* **Ensuring Metallurgical Ductility:** Proper thermal soaking dissolves carbide precipitates and equalizes internal core-to-surface grain structures, preventing surface tears, edge cracking, or cobbles during high-speed rolling of rebar, wire rod, or structural shapes.
* **Minimizing Surface Oxidation (Scale Loss):** A well-regulated furnace protects the steel surface from severe oxidation, ensuring minimal loss of saleable steel yield.

## 2. What Are the Different Types of Reheating Furnaces?

Reheating furnaces are categorized primarily by how steel stock is conveyed through the thermal chamber. Each type presents unique thermodynamic characteristics, maintenance requirements, and energy-saving opportunities during an audit.

### 1. Walking Beam Reheating Furnace
In a **[walking beam furnace](/furnaces/walking-beam-reheating-furnace)**, billets rest on stationary refractory hearths and are periodically lifted, advanced, and lowered onto the hearth by hydraulic walking beams.
* **Key Advantages:** Elimination of skid mark defects on the billet underside, top-and-bottom bilateral firing capability, excellent billet spacing flexibility, and zero physical scratching on steel surfaces.
* **Indonesian Market Application:** The standard choice for modern rolling lines in Cilegon and Surabaya rolling mills producing high-tensile rebar, SBQ steel, and wire rod.

### 2. Pusher-Type Reheating Furnace
In a pusher-type furnace, steel billets are charged end-to-end and mechanically shoved over water-cooled skid pipes by hydraulic pusher rams.
* **Key Advantages:** Simple mechanical construction and lower initial capital cost.
* **Drawbacks:** Severe skid mark chill spots on billets, high water-cooling thermal losses, billet buckling risks, and significant air infiltration around doors.
* **Indonesian Market Application:** Prevalent among older rolling mills commissioned between 1990 and 2015 throughout West and East Java.

### 3. Walking Hearth Furnace
Similar to walking beam furnaces, but moving refractory blocks form part of the hearth floor itself.
* **Key Advantages:** Fully refractory hearth without water-cooled skid pipes, eliminating bottom skid marks completely.
* **Indonesian Market Application:** Suited for heavy structural sections, blooms, and thick slabs.

### 4. Rotary Hearth Furnace
A circular doughnut-shaped furnace with a rotating refractory hearth carrying billets in an arc from charge door to discharge door.
* **Key Advantages:** Continuous individual billet handling and zone temperature control.
* **Indonesian Market Application:** Primarily utilized in seamless pipe plants, round billet heating, and forging operations.

## 3. How to Calculate Reheating Furnace Capacity?

Calculating nominal and actual reheating furnace capacity is an essential first step in any plant audit. Reheating capacity defines the balance between furnace throughput and rolling mill finishing speed.

### Reheating Furnace Capacity Calculation Formula
Nominal furnace throughput capacity (*C*, in metric tonnes per hour, t/h) is determined by the effective hearth surface area and the specific hearth loading rate:

```text
Capacity (t/h) = [Effective Hearth Area (m²) × Specific Hearth Loading Rate (kg/m²·h)] / 1,000
```

Where:
* **Effective Hearth Area (m²):** Internal Furnace Width (m) × Effective Heated Length (m).
* **Specific Hearth Loading Rate (kg/m²·h):** The rate at which the hearth can transfer sufficient heat into the steel charge:
  * **Top-fired pusher furnaces:** 350 to 480 kg/(m²·h)
  * **Top-and-bottom fired pusher furnaces:** 480 to 580 kg/(m²·h)
  * **Modern top-and-bottom fired walking beam furnaces:** 580 to 750 kg/(m²·h)

### Total Heat Input Capacity Calculation
To calculate the total thermal burner input (*Q_total*, in kcal/h or kW) required to sustain this production capacity:

```text
Q_total = Capacity (t/h) × Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC, kcal/tonne)
```

For example, a 60 t/h walking beam furnace operating at a baseline SFC of 380,000 kcal/t requires a burner firing capacity of:

```text
Q_total = 60 t/h × 380,000 kcal/t = 22,800,000 kcal/h (approx. 26.5 MW)
```

During our on-site audit, we contrast rated design capacity against **actual operational capacity** across different steel billet dimensions (e.g., 130 × 130 mm vs. 150 × 150 mm) to reveal bottlenecks in burner heat transfer and draft balance.

## 4. Reheating Furnace Audit in Indonesia: Regulatory Requirements (2018–2024 to 2026)

Indonesian industrial energy regulations have evolved rapidly over the past decade. Operating a steel reheating furnace without verified energy telemetry exposes mills to regulatory sanctions and severe gas price penalties.

### Regulatory Evolution: Indonesia Furnace Audit Mandates (2018–2026)
* **2018 (MEMR / ESDM Regulation No. 12/2018):** Introduced mandatory energy management protocols for industrial facilities consuming ≥ 6,000 TOE (tonnes of oil equivalent). Mills were required to designate a certified Energy Manager and conduct internal energy audits.
* **2022 (Government Regulation PP No. 33/2022 & ESDM 14/2022):** Expanded energy conservation requirements, tightening reporting standards and empowering inspectors to verify industrial thermal balance logs.
* **2023–2024 (HGBT Quota Realignment - ESDM Decrees):** Subsidized natural gas pricing under *Harga Gas Bumi Tertentu* (**USD 6.00 to 7.00 per MMBTU**) was formally tied to strict monthly quota allocations. Under ministerial adjustments across 2023 and 2024, consumption exceeding allocated quotas is invoiced at market rates (**USD 9.50 to 12.00 per MMBTU**)—instantly penalizing inefficient rolling mills with 50% to 70% cost surcharges.
* **2026 (Kemenperin Standar Industri Hijau - SIH Compliance):** Enforces mandatory Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC) caps per tonne of rolled steel, requiring verified carbon accounting, continuous flue gas emissions control, and waste heat recovery systems.

### Core Technical Requirements for an Indonesian Furnace Audit
To satisfy ESDM inspectors and Kemenperin SIH auditors, an industrial furnace audit must document:
1. **Calibrated Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC):** Gas consumption (in Nm³ or MMBTU) per metric tonne of heated steel across variable rolling speeds.
2. **Flue Gas Stack Diagnostics:** Continuous measurement of exit temperature, excess oxygen (O₂), carbon monoxide (CO), and NOₓ levels.
3. **Refractory Shell Loss Mapping:** Infrared thermographic scans of furnace walls, roof, hearth, and discharge doors to identify insulation degradation.
4. **Recuperator Heat Balance:** Preheated combustion air temperature vs. flue gas inlet/outlet enthalpy balance.
5. **Billet Discharge Temperature Uniformity:** Verification that billets achieve ±15°C core-to-surface temperature uniformity before entering the roughing mill.

## 5. Conducting a Rigorous Thermal Energy Audit: On-Site Methodology

You cannot manage what you do not measure. In many Indonesian steel mills, operators lack detailed data on their reheating furnace's actual thermodynamic performance. They monitor total gas intake and overall steel output, but the internal efficiency of individual heating zones remains an unmeasured "black box."

* **The Problem:** 
Legacy furnaces typically run on fixed air-to-fuel ratio control loops set during commissioning years ago. Over time, burner orifices wear out, gas composition fluctuates, and air dampers lose calibration. This leads to substantial heat losses. Without a professional audit, operators cannot pinpoint where energy is wasted, nor can they establish an accurate baseline to verify the financial return of an upgrade.

* **The Technical Principle:** 
A professional energy audit begins by mapping the furnace's heat balance. Using high-precision portable flue gas analyzers, thermal imaging cameras, and ultrasonic flow meters, engineers measure fuel gas velocity, combustion air flow, shell heat dissipation, and flue gas temperature. This data is used to calculate the Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC) curve across different production rates (tonnes per hour). This curve represents the thermodynamic baseline against which all future savings are measured.

* **FAQ Q&A:**
**Why is a professional thermal audit necessary before implementing furnace optimization?**
A professional thermal audit establishes a baseline for Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC) against varying production rates. Without this baseline, it is impossible to calculate actual fuel savings, measure ROI, or structure a performance-based contract. Additionally, audits identify specific areas of heat loss and oxygen levels, allowing engineers to custom-size recuperators and burner control zones.

* **Case Results in Cilegon, Banten:**
An energy audit conducted on a 60-tonne-per-hour pusher-type reheating furnace in Cilegon, Banten, revealed that the furnace was operating at an average thermal efficiency of just 42%. Flue gas was escaping at 810°C, and excess oxygen in the heating zone was measured at 6.8%. This excess air was carrying away an estimated 13.5% of the fuel's total chemical energy straight out of the stack.

## 6. Navigating HGBT Gas Quota Limits and SIH Compliance

The Indonesian government supports strategic industries, including steel, through the *Harga Gas Bumi Tertentu* (HGBT) policy, which caps natural gas prices at **USD 6.00 to 7.00 per MMBTU**. However, this pricing comes with strict operational constraints that make thermal efficiency a critical business survival factor.

* **The Problem:** 
HGBT gas allocations are subject to monthly consumption quotas set by the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM). If a steel mill exceeds its allocated monthly quota due to furnace inefficiencies or production delays, the excess gas must be purchased at market rates. These rates range from **USD 9.50 to 12.00 per MMBTU**—representing an immediate 50% to 70% price premium. This penalty can destroy a rolling mill's operating margin.

* **The Technical Principle:** 
Staying within HGBT quotas requires dynamic fuel tracking and smart furnace automation. A modern control system integrates the furnace's PLC with the rolling line's scheduling software. When a cobble occurs or the rolling mill pauses for roll changes, the automation system automatically triggers a predictive low-fire holding mode. Instead of keeping the furnace at full temperature, the system drops the burners to a minimum holding fire, preventing gas waste during idle periods.

* **FAQ Q&A:**
**How does furnace automation help steel mills stay within their monthly HGBT gas quotas?**
Indonesian steel mills under the HGBT policy receive subsidized gas but are subject to strict monthly allocation limits. Consumption above this quota is priced at premium market rates, increasing costs by up to 70%. Upgrading furnace automation introduces predictive holding modes that automatically throttle burners during mill delays, reducing overall gas usage by 8-10% and ensuring the mill stays within its quota limits.

* **Case Results in Surabaya, East Java:**
By implementing automated combustion zones and integrating predictive holding controls, a hot rolling mill in Surabaya, East Java, reduced its average monthly natural gas consumption by 9.2%. This efficiency gain allowed the mill to operate entirely within its HGBT allocation, saving an estimated USD 145,000 annually in market-rate gas penalties.

## 7. Waste Heat Recovery (WHR): Upgrading to High-Efficiency Recuperators

A significant portion of energy loss in a reheating furnace occurs through the flue gas. In conventional Indonesian mills, this hot exhaust gas is vented directly into the atmosphere, carrying valuable thermal energy with it.

* **The Problem:** 
Older steel reheating furnaces either lack waste heat recovery systems entirely or utilize outdated, single-pass recuperators that have degraded over time. Over years of thermal cycling, metallic recuperator tubes suffer from oxidation, thermal stress cracking, and weld failures. This results in combustion air leaking into the flue duct, reducing preheat temperatures and forcing the furnace to burn more gas to reach rolling temperatures.

* **The Technical Principle:** 
A high-efficiency waste heat recovery upgrade replaces degraded heat exchangers with modern, multi-pass metallic recuperators. These systems route incoming combustion air through high-temperature alloy tubes surrounded by hot flue gases. This preheats the combustion air to between 400°C and 450°C. Because the combustion air enters the burners already carrying substantial thermal energy, less natural gas is required to heat the air-fuel mixture to the flame temperature. For every 100°C increase in combustion air preheat, fuel consumption drops by approximately 5%.

* **Case Results in West Java:**
A structural steel rolling mill in West Java replaced a cracked single-pass recuperator with a double-pass metallic recuperator designed to T80 standards. The combustion air preheat temperature rose from an unstable 180°C to a consistent 420°C. This upgrade delivered a verified 11.5% reduction in natural gas consumption per tonne of steel rolled.

## 8. AI-Driven Zone-by-Zone Stoichiometric Combustion Control

In manual or semi-automated reheating furnaces, maintaining the correct ratio of air to natural gas is a major operational challenge. If there is too little air, fuel is wasted through incomplete combustion. If there is too much air, the excess oxygen cools the furnace chamber and accelerates billet oxidation.

* **The Problem:** 
Traditional control systems use a single mechanical linkage or a basic PLC loop to adjust air and gas valves simultaneously. This static approach cannot adapt to changes in gas pressure, combustion air temperature, or ambient humidity. As a result, furnaces typically run with high excess air levels (O₂ levels of 5% to 8%). This excess oxygen reacts with the hot steel billet surfaces to form iron oxide scale (scale loss), which flakes off during rolling, reducing the mill’s overall steel yield.

* **The Technical Principle:** 
AI-driven combustion control replaces static ratios with dynamic, zone-by-zone closed-loop regulation. Zirconium oxide (ZrO₂) oxygen sensors are installed in the flue duct of each heating zone. The automation system runs a predictive control algorithm that monitors real-time flue gas composition, zone temperatures, and billet charging speed. The system micro-adjusts the air and gas control valves in real-time, keeping excess oxygen levels tightly controlled between 1.5% and 2.0% across all zones.

* **Case Results in Jakarta Industrial Corridor:**
A rebar mill in the Jakarta area integrated zone-by-zone combustion control with zirconium oxide sensor feedback. The system maintained excess oxygen below 2.0% during steady-state operations. This reduced scale loss from 1.65% to 1.15%, saving approximately 5 tonnes of steel yield per day—delivering an additional USD 3,200 in daily product revenue.

High upfront costs and technical implementation risks often prevent steel mills from upgrading their furnaces. Mill managers worry that the projected savings may not materialize, leaving them with depreciating hardware and outstanding loans. This hesitation results in legacy furnaces running inefficiently for years, wasting hundreds of thousands of dollars in fuel.

* **The Technical Principle:** 
The **Energy Steward Model** (a performance-based contracting model) resolves this barrier by shifting all financial and technical risks to the technology provider.
