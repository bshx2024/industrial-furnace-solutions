## 1. What is a Reheating Furnace & What is Its Purpose in a Steel Mill?

Before diving into audit protocols, plant managers and operational engineers must understand the core thermodynamics and mechanical purpose of the reheating furnace within the rolling line.

### What is a Reheating Furnace?
A **reheating furnace** is a continuous or semi-continuous industrial furnace utilized in hot rolling mills to elevate solid semi-finished steel stock—such as continuously cast billets, blooms, beam blanks, or slabs—from ambient temperature (or hot-charging temperatures of 500°C to 700°C) to the plastic deformation range, typically **1,150°C to 1,250°C**.

Thermally, the furnace consists of three distinct functional chambers:
1. **Preheating Zone:** Utilizes counter-flowing convective heat from hot exhaust gases to raise billet temperature to ~700°C without consuming direct burner fuel.
2. **Heating Zone:** Operates at peak burner intensity (1,250°C–1,320°C chamber temperature) transferring radiant heat to the steel stock.
3. **Soaking Zone:** Operates under gentle, uniform radiant heat (1,180°C–1,220°C) allowing core-to-surface temperature equilibration across the billet cross-section.

### What is the Purpose of a Reheat Furnace in a Steel Mill?
The primary purpose of a reheat furnace in a steel mill is threefold:
* **Lowering Flow Stress & Yield Strength:** Steel at 1,200°C requires less than 15% of the mechanical rolling force needed at ambient temperatures, reducing the electric motor power consumption of roughing and intermediate stands and extending rolling roll lifespan. For a holistic plant-wide analysis, see our [Hot Rolling Mill Energy Efficiency Guide](/blog/hot-rolling-mill-energy-efficiency).
* **Ensuring Metallurgical Ductility:** Proper thermal soaking dissolves carbide precipitates and equalizes internal core-to-surface grain structures, preventing surface tears, edge cracking, or cobbles during high-speed rolling of rebar, wire rod, or structural shapes.
* **Minimizing Surface Oxidation (Scale Loss):** A well-regulated furnace protects the steel surface from severe oxidation, ensuring minimal loss of saleable steel yield.

## 2. What Are the Different Types of Reheating Furnaces & How to Calculate Capacity?

Reheating furnaces are categorized primarily by how steel stock is conveyed through the thermal chamber. Each type presents unique thermodynamic characteristics and energy-saving opportunities during an audit.

### Types of Reheating Furnaces
1. **[Walking Beam Reheating Furnace](/furnaces/walking-beam-reheating-furnace):** Billets rest on stationary refractory hearths and are periodically lifted and advanced by hydraulic walking beams. This eliminates skid mark chill spots and surface scratches, making it the preferred choice for modern rolling lines in Cilegon and Surabaya.
2. **Pusher-Type Reheating Furnace:** Billets are pushed end-to-end over water-cooled skid pipes. While mechanically simple, pusher furnaces suffer from severe skid mark chill spots, high water-cooling heat losses, and door air infiltration.
3. **Walking Hearth Furnace:** Moving refractory blocks form part of the hearth floor itself without water-cooled skid pipes, eliminating bottom skid marks for thick slabs or blooms.
4. **Rotary Hearth Furnace:** A rotating circular hearth carries billets from charge door to discharge door, primarily used for seamless pipe plants and forging rounds.

| Furnace Type | Conveyance Mechanism | Skid Mark Formation | Thermal Efficiency | Suitability for Retrofit & Auditing |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Walking Beam** | Moving hydraulic refractory beams | Negligible / None | Highest (45% – 58%) | Excellent for recuperator & AI zone control retrofits |
| **Pusher Type** | Hydraulic pusher ram over skid pipes | Severe along water skids | Moderate (32% – 42%) | Prime target for skid insulation and WHR upgrades |
| **Walking Hearth** | Stepped moving refractory hearth | None | High (40% – 50%) | Good for burner staging and air-fuel ratio automation |
| **Rotary Hearth** | Rotating circular hearth floor | None | Moderate-High (38% – 48%) | Ideal for oxygen trim and pressure control |

### How to Calculate Reheating Furnace Capacity?
Nominal furnace throughput capacity (*C*, in metric tonnes per hour, t/h) is determined by effective hearth surface area and specific hearth loading rate:

```text
Capacity (t/h) = [Effective Hearth Area (m²) × Specific Hearth Loading Rate (kg/m²·h)] / 1,000
```

Where:
* **Effective Hearth Area (m²):** Internal Furnace Width (m) × Effective Heated Length (m).
* **Specific Hearth Loading Rate (kg/m²·h):** 350–480 kg/(m²·h) for top-fired pusher furnaces; 480–580 kg/(m²·h) for top-and-bottom fired pusher furnaces; and 580–750 kg/(m²·h) for modern walking beam furnaces.
* **Total Thermal Input Capacity:** `Q_total = Capacity (t/h) × Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC, kcal/tonne)`. For example, a 60 t/h furnace at 380,000 kcal/t requires `60 × 380,000 = 22,800,000 kcal/h` (~26.5 MW) burner firing capacity.

During our on-site audit, we contrast rated design capacity against **actual operational capacity** across different steel billet dimensions (e.g., 130 × 130 mm vs. 150 × 150 mm) to reveal bottlenecks in burner heat transfer and draft balance.

## 3. Reheating Furnace Audit in Indonesia: Regulatory Requirements (2018–2024 to 2026)

Indonesian industrial energy regulations have evolved rapidly over the past decade. Operating a steel reheating furnace without verified energy telemetry exposes mills to regulatory sanctions and severe gas price penalties.

### Regulatory Evolution: Indonesia Furnace Audit Mandates (2018–2026)
* **2018 (MEMR / ESDM Regulation No. 12/2018):** Introduced mandatory energy management protocols for industrial facilities consuming ≥ 6,000 TOE (tonnes of oil equivalent). Mills were required to designate a certified Energy Manager and conduct internal energy audits.
* **2022 (Government Regulation PP No. 33/2022 & ESDM 14/2022):** Expanded energy conservation requirements, tightening reporting standards and empowering inspectors to verify industrial thermal balance logs.
* **2023–2024 (HGBT Quota Realignment - ESDM Decrees):** Subsidized natural gas pricing under *Harga Gas Bumi Tertentu* (**USD 6.00 to 7.00 per MMBTU**) was formally tied to strict monthly quota allocations. Under ministerial adjustments across 2023 and 2024, consumption exceeding allocated quotas is invoiced at market rates (**USD 9.50 to 12.00 per MMBTU**)—instantly penalizing inefficient rolling mills with 50% to 70% cost surcharges.
* **2026 (Kemenperin Standar Industri Hijau - SIH Compliance):** Enforces mandatory Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC) caps per tonne of rolled steel, requiring verified carbon accounting, continuous flue gas emissions control, and waste heat recovery systems.

### Core Technical Requirements for an Indonesian Furnace Audit
To satisfy ESDM inspectors and Kemenperin SIH auditors, an industrial furnace audit must document:
1. **Calibrated Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC):** Gas consumption (in Nm³ or MMBTU) per metric tonne of heated steel across variable rolling speeds.
2. **Flue Gas Stack Diagnostics:** Continuous measurement of exit temperature, excess oxygen (O₂), carbon monoxide (CO), and NOₓ levels.
3. **Refractory Shell Loss Mapping:** Infrared thermographic scans of furnace walls, roof, hearth, and discharge doors to identify insulation degradation.
4. **Recuperator Heat Balance:** Preheated combustion air temperature vs. flue gas inlet/outlet enthalpy balance.
5. **Billet Discharge Temperature Uniformity:** Verification that billets achieve ±15°C core-to-surface temperature uniformity before entering the roughing mill.

## 4. On-Site Audit Methodology & Key Thermal Losses

You cannot manage what you do not measure. In many Indonesian steel mills, operators lack detailed data on their reheating furnace's actual thermodynamic performance. They monitor total gas intake and overall steel output, but the internal efficiency of individual heating zones remains an unmeasured "black box."

A professional energy audit begins by mapping the furnace's heat balance. Using high-precision portable flue gas analyzers, thermal imaging cameras, and ultrasonic flow meters, engineers measure fuel gas velocity, combustion air flow, shell heat dissipation, and flue gas temperature. This data establishes the thermodynamic Specific Fuel Consumption (SFC) curve against varying production rates.

* **Case Results in Cilegon, Banten:**
An energy audit conducted on a 60-tonne-per-hour pusher-type reheating furnace in Cilegon, Banten, revealed that the furnace was operating at an average thermal efficiency of just 42%. Flue gas was escaping at 810°C, and excess oxygen in the heating zone was measured at 6.8%. This excess air was carrying away an estimated 13.5% of the fuel's total chemical energy straight out of the stack.

## 5. High-ROI Optimization: Waste Heat Recuperators & AI Combustion

To eliminate the heat losses identified during the audit, steel rolling mills implement two primary high-impact engineering retrofits:

### Waste Heat Recovery (WHR) via Multi-Pass Recuperators
A high-efficiency waste heat recovery upgrade replaces degraded heat exchangers with modern, multi-pass metallic recuperators. These systems route incoming combustion air through high-temperature alloy tubes surrounded by hot flue gases, preheating combustion air to 400°C–450°C. For every 100°C increase in combustion air preheat, fuel consumption drops by approximately 5%.
* **Case Results in West Java:** A structural steel rolling mill in West Java replaced a cracked single-pass recuperator with a double-pass metallic recuperator designed to T80 standards. Combustion air preheat rose from an unstable 180°C to a consistent 420°C, delivering a verified 11.5% reduction in natural gas consumption per tonne of steel rolled.

### AI-Driven Stoichiometric Combustion Control
AI-driven combustion control installs zirconium oxide (ZrO₂) oxygen sensors in the flue duct of each heating zone. A predictive algorithm micro-adjusts air and gas valves in real-time, keeping excess oxygen levels tightly controlled between 1.5% and 2.0% across all zones.
* **Case Results in Jakarta Industrial Corridor:** A rebar mill in the Jakarta area integrated zone-by-zone combustion control with zirconium oxide sensor feedback. Excess oxygen was maintained below 2.0% during steady-state operations, reducing scale loss from 1.65% to 1.15% and saving approximately 5 tonnes of steel yield per day ($3,200/day in additional product revenue).

## 6. ROI Analysis: The Zero CAPEX Energy Steward Model

Implementing deep thermal upgrades—including recuperators, PLC instrumentation, and zone control valves—typically requires an upfront capital investment of **USD 250,000 to USD 400,000** per furnace. For many Indonesian steel mills, allocating this level of CAPEX is difficult.

The **Energy Steward Model** (a performance-based contracting model) resolves this barrier. South Technology designs, procures, installs, and maintains all optimization equipment at **zero upfront cost** to the steel mill. Energy savings are measured monthly using the International Performance Measurement and Verification Protocol (IPMVP). The steel mill pays only a percentage (typically 50% to 60%) of verified fuel savings. If no savings are achieved, the mill pays nothing.

* **Case Results in Cilegon, Banten:**
A structural steel mill in Cilegon partnered with South Technology under the Energy Steward Model to retrofit its walking beam reheating furnace. South Technology invested USD 320,000 in a double-pass recuperator and AI combustion control. The project achieved a verified fuel consumption reduction of 11.8%. The resulting natural gas savings generated USD 42,000 in monthly cost reductions. The mill retained USD 18,900 of these savings monthly, improving its cash flow from day one without any capital expenditure.
* **Case Results in Surabaya, East Java:**
By implementing automated combustion zones and integrating predictive holding controls, a hot rolling mill in Surabaya, East Java, reduced its average monthly natural gas consumption by 9.2%. This efficiency gain allowed the mill to operate entirely within its HGBT allocation, saving an estimated USD 145,000 annually in market-rate gas penalties.
