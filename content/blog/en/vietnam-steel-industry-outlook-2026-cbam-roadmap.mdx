## Module 1: The CBAM Squeeze and Carbon Accounting
### The Problem: The €100/ton Carbon Cliff
Starting in 2026, the <a href="https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)</a> transitions from a reporting requirement to a financial obligation. For Vietnamese steel exporters, this means purchasing carbon certificates for every ton of embedded CO2. With EU carbon prices hovering between €60-€100 per ton, an inefficient mill could see its export costs rise by $150-$200 per ton, effectively wiping out profit margins.

### The Principle: Digital Carbon Footprint Verification
Carbon accounting at the reheating stage requires high-fidelity data on fuel consumption per burner zone and oxygen levels in the flue gas. Traditional gas meters provide only aggregate data, which CBAM verifiers may reject or penalize with "default values" (usually the worst-performing 10% of EU producers).

**How does EcoReheating help with CBAM verification?**
Our [AI Combustion System](/solutions) integrates real-time flow meters and oxygen analyzers to create a "Digital Twin" of your carbon emissions. This data is not only used to optimize fuel use but serves as a primary evidence dossier for CBAM reporting, ensuring you are taxed on your actual (lower) emissions rather than industry averages.

## Module 2: Full-Fiber Roof Technology for Thermal Inertia
### The Problem: The "Refractory Anchor"
Most older furnaces in Vietnam use heavy refractory castables or bricks for the roof. These materials have high thermal mass, meaning they absorb massive amounts of energy during startup and continue to radiate heat long after the furnace should have cooled. This "thermal inertia" makes the furnace sluggish to respond to production changes and leads to high standby fuel consumption.

### The Principle: Low-Mass Ceramic Fiber Modules
We replace traditional heavy roofs with high-purity ceramic fiber modules. These modules are factory-compressed and installed in a "stack-bond" pattern. Because the fiber has 1/10th the density of traditional refractories, the roof stores very little heat.

**Why is the Full-Fiber Roof superior for multi-product mills?**
In mills that switch between different steel grades or sizes, the furnace needs to change temperature frequently. A fiber roof allows for rapid heating and cooling cycles, reducing "dead time" between batches. In our project at [Jincheng Metal](/hero-cases), the fiber roof retrofit reduced startup fuel consumption by 25%.

> "By reducing the thermal weight of the furnace structure, we turn a sluggish industrial giant into a responsive, high-precision thermal instrument."
> — Zhang Liang, Senior Process Engineer, South Technology

## Module 3: AI-Driven Smart Combustion Optimization
### The Problem: The "Excess Air" Thief
In manual or PLC-based control systems, operators often maintain a high "excess air" ratio to ensure complete combustion and prevent smoke. However, every cubic meter of excess air must be heated to furnace temperature (up to 1250°C) only to be blown out the chimney. This is the single largest source of fuel waste in industrial furnaces.

### The Principle: Predictive Stoichiometric Control
Our AI system uses mechanism models to predict the required heat for incoming billets based on their dimensions and metallurgy. It then adjusts the air-fuel ratio at each burner zone to maintain the stoichiometric "sweet spot."

**How does AI perform better than a veteran operator?**
A human operator can only adjust a few zones every 15-30 minutes. Our AI adjusts 20+ zones every few seconds, reacting to changes in gas pressure, air temperature, and mill pacing that are invisible to the naked eye. This [intelligent reheating](/solutions) typically adds another 5% in fuel savings on top of hardware upgrades.

## Module 4: Oxidation Control and Yield Improvement
### The Problem: Turning Steel into Dust
When steel is heated in an oxygen-rich atmosphere, the surface reacts to form "scale" (iron oxide). This scale falls off during rolling, representing a direct loss of saleable material. In many Vietnamese mills, scale loss is as high as 1.2%. For a 500,000 ton per year mill, this means 6,000 tons of steel are literally turned into dust.

### The Principle: Atmosphere-Zone Management
By using [non-ceramic high-emissivity coatings](/solutions) and precise AI air-fuel control, we create a "reducing" or neutral atmosphere in the high-heat zones. This inhibits the chemical reaction that forms scale.

**How does a 0.2% yield improvement impact ROI?**
While fuel savings are important, yield improvement is often the "hidden gold." Reducing scale loss from 1.0% to 0.8% adds 1,000 tons of saleable steel per year for a mid-sized mill. At current steel prices, this yield gain alone can pay for the entire optimization project in less than 12 months.

## FAQ Section

**Why does combustion optimization reduce fuel consumption in reheating furnaces?**
Combustion optimization adjusts the air-fuel ratio in real-time across each burner zone, eliminating excess air that carries heat out through the flue. By maintaining optimal stoichiometric conditions, furnaces achieve 7-15% fuel savings while improving temperature uniformity.

**How does the Zero CAPEX model work?**
EcoReheating deploys all hardware, software, and engineering at zero upfront cost. The steel mill pays only a share of verified energy savings, measured against an agreed baseline. If there are no savings, there is no payment.

**What is the impact of CISA T80 technology on steel quality?**
The T80 standard, developed by the China Iron and Steel Association, focuses on "Extreme Efficiency." Beyond fuel savings, it ensures tighter temperature bands and reduced oxidation, which directly improves surface quality and reduces internal stresses in the rolled product.
