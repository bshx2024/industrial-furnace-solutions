## Technical Module 1: AI Stoichiometric Control & Flue Gas Optimization
### The Physics: Stoichiometric vs. Lean Combustion
In traditional Vietnamese mill operations, "lean combustion" (excess air) is used as a safety buffer. However, every 1% of excess oxygen above the stoichiometric ideal represents a 1.5% loss in fuel efficiency. At 1200°C, heating unnecessary air is the largest single source of waste.

### The T80 Solution: Predictive Zone Modulation
The T80 roadmap replaces static air-fuel ratios with AI-driven zone modulation. By monitoring flue gas composition at the zone level and cross-referencing it with the furnace’s thermal load, the system maintains O2 levels below 2% without risking incomplete combustion (CO formation).

**Why is this critical for CBAM?**
CBAM verifiers prioritize facilities with real-time combustion monitoring. By capturing the exact gas-to-air ratio every 10 seconds, your mill generates a verifiable "Carbon Passport" that proves lower intensity, avoiding the punitive default tax values applied to non-instrumented mills.

## Technical Module 2: The "Low Thermal Mass" Refractory Shift
### The Problem: Thermal Inertia in Castable Roofs
Most furnaces in Vietnam use heavy castable refractories. While durable, these materials store massive amounts of heat (thermal mass). When the rolling mill stops for an hour, the furnace continues to burn fuel just to maintain the temperature of the roof.

### The T80 Solution: Multi-Layer Ceramic Fiber Modules
We transition the furnace roof to high-purity ceramic fiber. Because the density is 1/10th of traditional refractories, the roof stores 85% less heat. This allows the furnace to respond to production delays by dropping to a low-fuel "holding state" almost instantly, and recovering to rolling temperature in minutes rather than hours.

## Technical Module 3: High-Emissivity Functional Coatings
### The Principle: Infrared Radiation Capture
Inside a reheating furnace, 90% of the heat transfer to the steel billet happens via radiation. Traditional refractories lose emissivity (the ability to radiate heat) as they age. 

### The T80 Solution: Non-Ceramic High-Emissivity Coating
We apply a proprietary high-emissivity coating to the interior walls and hearth. This coating reflects and re-radiates infrared energy back into the billet. This allows the furnace to achieve the same billet core temperature with a 30-50°C lower furnace gas temperature, directly reducing fuel consumption by 3-5%.

## Technical Module 4: Walking Hearth Optimization (Localized for Vietnam)
### The Challenge: The "Bottom-Side" Temperature Gap
Many mills in Vietnam operate legacy walking hearth furnaces where the billet sits directly on the refractory. This prevents heat from reaching the bottom surface, leading to uneven rolling and higher motor loads.

### The T80 Solution: AI-Compensated Hearth Profiling
For mills that cannot afford a full conversion to walking beam, T80 provides an AI-compensated approach. By dynamically adjusting the bottom-side side burners based on the hearth’s thermal absorption model, we can reduce the temperature delta between top and bottom surfaces by 60%, significantly improving rolling consistency and yield.
